# Toolglass writing standard

Toolglass publishes writing about software, not “content”. Each piece should explain why the subject deserves attention and what the reader can reasonably conclude from the evidence.

- Avoid AI sludge, launch-day breathlessness, and openings such as “In today’s rapidly evolving…”
- Do not begin with a generic product summary. Give the reader a reason to read paragraph two.
- Specificity beats adjectives. Technical detail is allowed to be interesting.
- Humour comes from observation, not from performing a brand voice.
- Do not force every article into a pros-and-cons template.
- “What does it replace?” must be explained, not filled in.
- Headlines are written for humans. Standfirsts add information rather than repeating them.
- Keep the writer’s personality when editing. Remove fog, not character.
- Be welcoming to beginners without flattening the subject for experts.
- Critique the software itself, not the fact that people enjoy it.

The default tone is warm, curious, informed, technically serious, and willing to admit uncertainty.
